## Watching the Giants of the Canopy: The Great Blue Heron Rookeries at Sailor Bar

The **Great Blue Heron rookeries** located within the mature trees of [Sailor Bar Park](https://regionalparks.saccounty.gov/content/regionalparks/us/en/parks/american-river-parkway/sailor-bar.html) provide birdwatchers and nature enthusiasts with a **magnificent, active nesting spectacle** along the American River Parkway. Recognized by the [Friends of Sailor Bar](/friends-of-sailor-bar) as a prime wildlife viewing highlight, these communal nesting sites (rookeries) host multiple large nests high in the cottonwoods and oaks.

## The Nesting Experience

Great blue herons are colonial nesters, meaning they prefer to build their large, stick-platform nests close together in high tree canopies where they feel protected from ground predators. Visitors walking the trails near the river or the fishing pond can often look up to spot these impressive birds tending to their young, preening, or engaging in elaborate courtship displays during breeding season. Their deep, harsh croaks echo through the trees, making them as easy to hear as they are to spot.

## What to Look For

- **Active Season:** Spring is the peak time for nesting activity, egg-laying, and feeding hungry chicks, though adult herons can be seen lingering near the water year-round.
- **Hunting Habits:** Beyond the canopy rookeries, you can routinely watch these stately birds stalking fish, amphibians, and small rodents along the shallow edges of the [Sacramento Audubon Society](https://www.sacramentoaudubon.org/sas-birding-locations/sailor-bar) birding hotspots.
- **Viewing Tips:** Bring binoculars or a telephoto camera lens so you can observe the nests from a respectful distance without disturbing the birds.

## Coexisting Responsibly

Because rookeries are sensitive nesting grounds, visitors are encouraged to stay on designated trails and avoid making loud noises directly beneath active trees. Respecting these boundaries ensures the herons return year after year to raise their next generations above the riverbanks.
