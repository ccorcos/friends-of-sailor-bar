The diverse plant life documented on the [Sailor Bar Biodiversity Project on iNaturalist](https://www.inaturalist.org/projects/sailor-bar-biodiversity) spans several distinct ecological zones, from moist riverbanks to dry upland gravel tailings.

Here are the key plant species you can find across these habitats:

### Riparian Trees and Canopy

- **Fremont Cottonwood (*Populus fremontii*)**: The largest fast-growing tree dominating the river’s edge, creating crucial shade and structural canopy for the ecosystem.
- **Willows (*Salix spp.*)**: Plentiful along the floodplains and shoreline, these trees help stabilize the banks against erosion and thrive in moist soils.
- **White Alder (*Alnus rhombifolia*)**: Stands of alder line the waterways, contributing to the dense, shaded riparian corridors that cool the riverbanks.

### Oak Woodland Staples

- **Valley Oak (*Quercus lobata*) & Blue Oak (*Quercus douglasii*)**: Found slightly further back from the water where the soil improves, these long-lived oaks anchor the park’s woodlands.
- **Coyote Brush (*Baccharis pilularis*)**: A resilient, evergreen native shrub that dominates the transitions between the woodlands and open grasslands.
- [**Blue Elderberry (*Sambucus cerulea*)**: A large, deciduous shrub that can grow to 6 metres (20 ft) in height and width.](/archive/the-elderberry)

### Wildflowers and Ground Cover

- **Mountain Garland (*Clarkia unguiculata*)**: A beautiful native wildflower that paints the open fields and dirt paths with pink and purple blooms during the late spring. [[1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OtkVLXMWPhg)]
- **Yerba Mansa (*Anemopsis californica*)**: A native perennial found in the damp seeps and pond edges, recognized by its distinct cone-shaped white flowers that turn red in the fall.

### Invasive Species of Concern

- **Yellow Star-Thistle (*Centaurea solstitialis*)**: A highly aggressive, non-native weed that blankets the dry gravel tailings, outcompeting native grasses.
- **Red Water Fern (*Azolla filiculoides*)**: An opportunistic species that occasionally forms thick, carpet-like layers over stagnant waters, such as the park’s Turtle Pond.
