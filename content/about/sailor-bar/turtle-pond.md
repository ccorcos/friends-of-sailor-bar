## Discover Turtle Pond at Sailor Bar: A Peaceful Haven in Fair Oaks

Tucked away at the northern end of [Sailor Bar Park](https://regionalparks.saccounty.gov/content/regionalparks/us/en/parks/american-river-parkway/sailor-bar.html) along the American River Parkway, Turtle Pond offers visitors a serene and scenic escape from the busier stretches of the river. Known officially by Sacramento County as the Sailor Bar Pond, this quiet body of water has become a favorite destination for Fair Oaks locals looking to fish, birdwatch, or simply enjoy a quiet stroll.

## The Creation of Turtle Pond

The pond was originally created in the late 1940’s while the Fair Oaks Gravel Company was operating at Sailor Bar. The companies’ night watchman and his family lived in a small 900 square foot cottage located in the oak woodland area between where the pond is now and the parking lot. At the time there was an intermittent creek (instead of a pond) that was fed from the ravines that drain into the area. Since the Lewis family had a pig pen located on the other side of the intermittent creek, they would have to ford through the creek to feed the animals when it rained. To make it easier to care for their animals, on Sundays when the Fair Oaks Gravel Company was closed, Amos and his son Ken used the company’s equipment to construct a berm across the creek. Over time, turtle pond eventually formed. The old cottage was removed by Regional Parks after the property became part of the American River Parkway.

## Wildlife and Birdwatching

True to its name, Turtle Pond is home to a robust population of turtles, particularly red-eared sliders, which can routinely be spotted sunning themselves on half-submerged logs. Beyond its reptilian residents, the pond acts as a vibrant wetland habitat that attracts a rich variety of birdlife. Depending on the season, birdwatchers can observe mallards, pied-billed grebes, red-winged blackbirds, and red-shouldered hawks nesting among the cattails or hunting from the overhead oak canopy. Local beavers and river otters are also known to make occasional appearances along the water’s edge.

## Activities and Recreation

The calm, current-free water makes Turtle Pond a highly accessible spot for casual shore fishing. Anglers frequently visit the banks to cast for largemouth bass, bluegill, redear, and green sunfish. Because of its gentle terrain, the short loop trail circling the pond is excellent for families, photographers, and visitors looking for an easy, flat walk rather than a strenuous hike.

## Planning Your Visit

- **Access:** The pond is located just north of the main parking area at the Illinois Avenue entrance (4253 Illinois Ave, Fair Oaks, CA). From the parking lot, it is a short, flat walk to the water’s edge.
- **Fees:** There is a standard $8 daily vehicle parking fee to enter Sacramento County Regional Parks, payable via self-registration stations at the entrance.
- **Regulations:** Visitors planning to fish should be aware that the area is subject to California state fishing regulations.

Whether you are looking to introduce children to the local wildlife or want a quiet place to unwind at sunset, Turtle Pond is an essential highlight of the Sailor Bar experience.
